Urdu Voice Benchmark

Will an AI voice agent understand your Pakistani callers?

Three speech recognition engines and four Azure Urdu voices, tested on everyday Pakistani Urdu recorded on ordinary phones. Here is what a business owner should know before putting one on the phone line.

Caller says صفر تین چار پانچ، چھ سات آٹھ نو صفر ایک دو
Should be 0345 6789012
Engine writes 6789012 0345

Every digit is right, and the number is still wrong. Nothing in the text looks suspicious, so the agent books the appointment under a number that doesn't exist.

The short answer

Ordinary sentences are understood well. On greetings, business talk and English–Urdu mixing, the best engine gets about 93 to 97 of every 100 letters right.
Phone numbers are not. Even the best engine got only 33 of 53 phone numbers fully right. Never let an agent take a number by voice alone.
A free engine is the most accurate. Whisper large-v3 (open source) made fewer mistakes than Azure. Azure is still better at casual speech and is faster today.
Most voice problems come from how the text is written. Spelling, times and codes decide how the voice sounds. Several fixes cost nothing (see the checklist).

Azure has no Pakistani Urdu model

Its only Urdu speech recognition is Indian Urdu. You can see it in the output: rupee amounts come back with the Indian ₹ sign, and half past seven comes back as 7.5. Clean these up before your agent's AI reads them.

Speaking to callers

Eight Pakistani listeners rated 24 sentences each without knowing which voice they were hearing: 192 ratings in total.

Male voices sounded more natural. Uzma had a wrong-sounding word in 27% of clips, the most of any voice.

The "Pakistani" and "Indian" labels made no difference to listeners. Only 3% of ratings heard an Indian accent, and most of those were on the Pakistani voices. What listeners did notice were particular words:

Checklist before you launch

Most of these cost nothing: they change only the instructions your agent's AI follows.

  • Take phone numbers on the keypad, or read them back

    Read the number back in groups and ask the caller to confirm, whichever engine you use.

  • Use a male voice

    Azure Asad, with Salman as the fallback.

  • Write English words in Urdu script, with Pakistani spelling

    Every voice was clearer this way. The spelling decides the vowels:

    اپوائنٹمنٹnotاپائنٹمنٹ
  • Write times as words

    Written as digits, every voice read 7:30 as "seven baj kar tees minute baje".

    ساڑھے سات بجےnot7:30
  • Write booking codes digit by digit

    Otherwise 4721 is read as "forty-seven twenty-one".

    چار سات دو ایکnot4721
  • Add pronunciation rules for problem words

    Fix words like یا and جزاک اللہ, and listen to them before going live.

  • Listen to a real call to the end

    One listener heard the last words of sentences fading. Don't put the key detail in the final word.

How it was tested

Sentences
238 in daily-use Pakistani Urdu: clinic, shop, property, office and customer-service talk, with English words, Arabic phrases and numbers
Speakers
6, on their own phones, speakerphone at arm's length
Engines
Azure Speech (Indian Urdu), Whisper large-v3, Whisper turbo
Voices

Listeners

Not measured
Speed on a live call, close-microphone audio, voices from other providers

Six speakers and eight listeners are enough to show the main trends, but not small differences. Full method, numbers and limitations are in the public repository.
